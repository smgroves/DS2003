# Project 1 Presentations

After submitting your written report, you will present your analysis to the class in a short presentation. This is your chance to walk an audience through your best insight — not to read your report out loud.

## Format

- **Time limit: 5 minutes, strictly enforced.** You will be cut off at 5 minutes whether or not you're finished, so rehearse with a timer before class.
- Present directly from your HTML report or notebook (screen share) — no separate slide deck is required, though you're welcome to build one if you'd rather present that way.
- **[Instructor: fill in presentation order/sign-up mechanism, and whether there is separate Q&A time after the 5 minutes.]**

## Suggested Structure

Five minutes is not enough time to walk through everything in your report — pick your best material rather than trying to cover it all. A structure that tends to work:

| Time | Content |
|---|---|
| ~30 sec | What's the dataset, and why did you choose it? |
| ~30 sec | Your most important research question(s) |
| ~3 min | Walk through your **2–3 best visualizations** — the ones with the most important or surprising insight, not a tour of every chart you made |
| ~30 sec | One takeaway or lesson learned from the analysis |
| ~30 sec | Buffer for transitions/nerves |

## Tips

- **Pick your best insight, not your first one.** The chart that took you three iterations to get right is usually more interesting to present than the one you made first.
- **Explain the "why," not just the "what."** Don't just describe what's on the chart — say what you want the audience to notice, and why you chose that encoding to show it.
- **Don't read your captions aloud.** Your caption was written for a silent reader going at their own pace; a live audience needs you to point at the chart and talk them through it in real time.
- **Practice out loud with a timer.** Five minutes disappears fast once you're standing up talking — most people's first run-through goes long.

## Grading

The presentation is graded separately from the written report, worth **[INSTRUCTOR: insert point value, e.g. 20 points]**.

| Component | Excellent | Satisfactory | Poor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insight Selection** | Chose the most compelling finding(s) from the analysis; clearly prioritized rather than trying to cover everything. | Covered reasonable content, but didn't clearly prioritize the strongest insight over weaker ones. | Presented trivial or arbitrary content with no sense of what mattered most. |
| **Visual Communication** | Walked the audience through each chart's encoding and what to notice; the audience could follow the insight without having read the report. | Explained charts adequately, but leaned on captions or assumed background the audience didn't have. | Showed charts without explaining them, or the audience couldn't tell what the chart was showing. |
| **Structure & Clarity** | Clear beginning (context/question), middle (evidence), and end (takeaway); easy to follow start to finish. | Generally organized, but some transitions were unclear or the throughline was hard to follow. | Disorganized or rambling; no clear structure. |
| **Time Management** | Finished within the 5-minute limit with time used purposefully, no rushing or padding. | Ran slightly over/under, or paced unevenly (e.g., rushed the ending). | Significantly over/under time, or clearly unrehearsed. |
| **Delivery & Engagement** | Confident, audience-facing delivery; engaged the room rather than reading off the screen. | Adequate delivery, but relied heavily on reading from notes/screen. | Difficult to follow due to delivery (e.g., inaudible, entirely read from a script). |
