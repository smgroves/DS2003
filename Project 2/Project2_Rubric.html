Project 2 Rubric

Interactive notebook + infographic or science visualization. 200 points total (20% of the course grade). Rubric cells may not map exactly to specific point scores.

DeliverablePoints
1. Proposal25
2. Final-piece sketches + critique25
3. Final presentation50
4. Final submission: interactive notebook (50) + infographic / science visualization (50)100

1. Proposal (25 pts)

ComponentExcellentSatisfactoryPoor
Dataset7 pts Source, collection method, size, and key variables are described; the dataset is substantial and not a toy dataset. Dataset is appropriate, but the description is missing its source, size, or variables. Dataset is trivial or built-in, or not described.
Research questions8 pts Three or more specific, answerable questions that go beyond single summary statistics. Questions are reasonable but vague or shallow. Fewer than three questions, or questions the data can't answer.
Audience, track & interactions7 pts Names a specific audience and track, and proposes interactions with a clear reason a static chart wouldn't be enough. Audience or interactions named, but generic ("the public," "add a dropdown"). Missing audience, track, or interaction ideas.
Team plan3 pts Every member has a clear lead role. Roles missing or unbalanced.

2. Final-piece sketches + critique (25 pts)

ComponentExcellentSatisfactoryPoor
Thumbnails & rough draft10 pts Two or more genuinely different layouts plus a rough draft of the strongest one, developed enough to critique. Only one layout, layouts that differ only slightly, or no rough draft. Missing or too rough to critique.
Takeaway & audience7 pts A specific one-sentence takeaway and a named audience, both reflected in the layouts. Takeaway or audience stated, but vague or disconnected from the sketches. Missing.
Critique participation & follow-up8 pts Gave specific, constructive feedback to other groups; submitted a feedback list with a concrete plan for each item. Participated, but the feedback given or the follow-up plan was thin. Absent, or no feedback list submitted.

3. Final presentation (50 pts)

ComponentExcellentSatisfactoryPoor
Live demo15 pts Uses the interactions live to reveal the key insights; the audience sees why interactivity matters. Shows the notebook, but scrolls through cells rather than using the interactions purposefully. No demo, or the notebook breaks during the presentation.
Final piece draft13 pts A near-final draft whose takeaway and design choices are explained. An early draft, or shown without explanation. Missing.
Structure & time11 pts Clear context → evidence → takeaway; finishes within 10 minutes. Generally organized, but ran over or rushed. Disorganized, or significantly over time.
Delivery & team11 pts Every member speaks; confident and audience-facing. Uneven speaking roles, or delivery read from the screen. One person presents everything, or hard to follow.

4. Final submission (100 pts)

4a. Interactive notebook (50 pts)

ComponentExcellentSatisfactoryPoor
About section8 pts Engaging introduction to the data, its source and limitations, and the research questions; static charts use explanatory techniques (active titles, annotation, highlighting). Data and questions introduced, but limitations are missing or the static charts are exploratory-style with no emphasis. Missing or reads like lab notes.
Answers to research questions10 pts At least three charts meaningfully answer the research questions, and each is followed by an interpretation of what to notice and what it means. Charts address the questions, but interpretations just describe the chart rather than explain it. Fewer than three charts, or charts unrelated to the questions.
Interaction design12 pts Uses ipywidgets controls and Plotly micro-interactions, each with a clear purpose, stated in a note on what it lets the reader do that a static chart couldn't. Linked views or other thoughtful touches earn full marks. Requirements met, but some interactions add little (redundant hover text, a dropdown with no meaningful choices). Missing ipywidgets or Plotly, or interactions that get in the way of reading the chart.
Visual design8 pts Effective chart types and encodings; clear titles, labels, and units; formatted hover text; accessible color. Mostly effective, with a few encoding or labeling errors. Poorly chosen chart types or encodings; unlabeled axes; raw column names.
Narrative & takeaway6 pts Reads like a short article a newcomer could follow; ends with a clear main takeaway. Mostly followable, but the transitions or the takeaway are weak. No narrative thread; no takeaway.
Reproducibility6 pts Runs top-to-bottom in a fresh kernel with no errors; data (or a download script), requirements, and HTML backup included. Runs after minor fixes (a path, a missing package). Does not run, or data is missing.

4b. Infographic / science visualization (50 pts)

ComponentExcellentSatisfactoryPoor
Main takeaway12 pts A single, clear takeaway a reader gets within seconds; stands on its own without the notebook. Takeaway present, but competing with secondary points or requiring effort to find. No clear takeaway; reads like a collage of charts.
Audience fit & genre10 pts Infographic: plain language and a headline a general reader understands. Science vis: labeled panels and a complete figure legend (sample sizes, error bars defined) at journal standard. Mostly fits the audience, but uses jargon (infographic) or has an incomplete legend (science vis). Wrong level for the audience, or ignores the conventions of the genre.
Composition & visual design12 pts Clear visual hierarchy and reading order; consistent alignment, type, and color; effective encodings; accessible color. Generally well designed, with some clutter, misalignment, or competing emphasis. Cluttered or confusing layout; ineffective encodings.
Data integrity7 pts Data marks generated from code; source cited on the graphic; no deceptive encodings; uncertainty shown where relevant. Minor issues (source hard to find, a questionable axis choice). Misleading encodings, hand-drawn data marks, or no source.
Design rationale & revision9 pts Rationale explains audience, takeaway, and three design decisions; the revision clearly responds to presentation feedback; contribution statement included. Rationale present but thin, or the revisions are minor. No rationale, or no evidence of revision.
